Personal Essay

Speed, Stories and Sound

Written by Aniket Raj • On Music, Literature & Formula 1

Many things shape a person: family, friendships, places, experiences, mistakes and even the different phases of life. My interests are only one part of what has made me the person I am today, but they still hold an important place in my life. Among them, music, literature and Formula 1 stand out. Music has accompanied me for most of my life, reading is a relatively recent discovery, and Formula 1 brings excitement, unpredictability and, as a Ferrari supporter, a fair amount of frustration.

01 / Sound — Beyond Earphones

Music has probably played the longest role in my life. Describing my taste is difficult because it has never remained limited to one language, period or genre. I enjoy ghazals and Bollywood music from the 1970s, 80s and 90s. Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, Begum Akhtar, Ghulam Ali and Jagjit Singh are some of the artists I frequently return to. At the same time, I also enjoy English music, particularly songs such as Hotel California, Stairway to Heaven and The House of the Rising Sun. Occasionally, I listen to rock and jazz, and recently I have also started exploring French music.

What I listen to usually depends on my mood. Sometimes the lyrics matter most, but at other times a voice, a guitar solo or simply the atmosphere of a song is enough to stay with me. I think that is what makes music so personal. Two people can listen to the same song and carry completely different feelings away from it.

My relationship with music has also gone beyond listening. I first learned the harmonium and later moved on to the tabla. After that, I taught myself to play the ukulele and harmonica. I am not an expert in any of these instruments, but becoming an expert was never really the point. Learning them allowed me to participate in music rather than experience it only through earphones. Music has stayed with me through good days, difficult days and completely ordinary ones. It provides comfort without asking me to explain why I need it.

“Music gives me comfort and a way to connect with emotions. Literature introduces me to minds and lives beyond my own. Formula 1 gives me excitement and reminds me how quickly circumstances can change.”

02 / Stories — From White Nights to Crime and Punishment

Literature entered my life much later. I started reading seriously towards the end of 2024, with Fyodor Dostoevsky’s White Nights. I did not expect such a short work to affect me so deeply, but it made me realise that a story does not need to be long to leave behind something meaningful. The loneliness, hope and emotional vulnerability in the novella made reading feel more personal than I had previously imagined.

Since then, I have explored different genres, from classics and philosophical fiction to mysteries and thrillers. Each genre offers a different experience, but the books that stay with me most are usually those that make me question a character, an idea or even myself. I am now around three hundred pages into Dostoevsky’s Crime and Punishment. In a way, the movement from White Nights to Crime and Punishment reflects my journey as a reader: I began with a short and emotionally direct novella, and I am now attempting something much longer, darker and more complex.

After coming to JNU, I have not been able to give reading as much time as I would like. Classes, assignments and everyday university life frequently take over. Still, even when I am not reading consistently, I do not feel that I have moved away from books. Reading has already changed the way I spend time alone. It gives me access to thoughts, lives and situations that I may never encounter personally. A book does not always give clear answers, but it can leave me with better questions.

03 / Speed — Strategy, Speed & Scuderia Ferrari

Formula 1 is the newest of these interests. I began following it seriously during the later part of the 2024 season. I was initially attracted by the speed, but I soon realised that the sport involves much more than fast cars. Engineering, strategy, teamwork, driver rivalries and decisions made within seconds can completely change the result of a race. A driver may appear to be competing alone on the track, but every performance depends on hundreds of people working behind the scenes.

Supporting Ferrari has also taught me to control my expectations. A promising free-practice session may be followed by disappointing qualifying, a difficult Grand Prix or a strategy decision that leaves supporters wondering what happened. Sometimes the drivers produce exciting and clean battles. At other times, fighting each other can cost the team valuable positions. Following Ferrari can be frustrating, but the frustration is also part of the attachment. Even after a terrible weekend, I return for the next race with the hope that everything might finally come together.

Music, literature and Formula 1 do not define my entire personality. I have been shaped by many people, experiences and circumstances beyond them. However, these interests have still contributed something meaningful. They represent sound, stories and speed, but more importantly, they represent three genuine parts of my life. I may not always have enough time for them, and my relationship with each may change as I grow older. Still, each has left something behind in the person I am becoming.
